# WingstopMenus Content and SEO Rules

These instructions apply to every page, article, comparison, review, FAQ, menu guide, and content edit in this repository.

Last guidance review: 2026-08-03.

## 1. Authority and Conflict Order

When two recommendations conflict, follow this order:

1. Current Google Search Essentials, spam policies, structured-data policies, and Search Central documentation.
2. Accurate, first-party information from Wingstop, regulators, government agencies, or the original data owner.
3. The reader's needs and the page's real search intent.
4. Current Semrush guidance as a research and editorial aid.
5. Tool scores, competitor patterns, keyword counts, and stylistic preferences.

Google and Semrush guidance can change. Before a major new content batch, a review page, or a time-sensitive update, check the current official documentation. Record the access date in the internal research notes. Never describe a rule as current without checking it when it may have changed.

Semrush is a useful secondary source, not a Google ranking-policy authority. Do not turn a Semrush recommendation, Writing Assistant score, keyword range, or readability score into a guaranteed ranking rule.

## 2. People-First Standard

Create each page for a real Wingstop customer with a specific task. The page should help the reader choose, compare, calculate, verify, or order without returning to search for the missing answer.

Every page must:

- answer one clear primary intent;
- provide original organization, analysis, or practical utility beyond rewriting sources;
- use verifiable facts and state important limitations;
- distinguish national facts from store-specific prices, promotions, availability, and preparation;
- stay within this site's menu, flavor, nutrition, allergen, ordering, deal, and location focus;
- receive individual research and editing instead of mass template generation.

Do not create a page because a keyword exists. Do not create many near-duplicate pages for query variations, cities, modifiers, or AI fan-out questions. Google treats scaled, low-value content made to manipulate rankings as spam, regardless of whether a person or AI produced it.

## 3. No Arbitrary Length or SEO Quotas

Google does not have a preferred word count. Use the shortest length that fully satisfies the reader's task and covers necessary evidence, exceptions, and decisions.

Do not require:

- a maximum or minimum article word count;
- exactly 5, 10, or any fixed number of facts;
- an exact keyword density;
- a fixed number of headings, FAQs, links, or sources;
- passive voice below an arbitrary percentage;
- every section to use the same paragraph or answer length.

Depth comes from useful coverage, not bulk. Remove repeated definitions, generic history, obvious filler, and sections added only to make an article longer.

## 4. Research and Fact Verification

Research before drafting. Build a claim ledger that records:

- the claim;
- its source URL or local official document;
- access or publication date;
- whether it is national, local, estimated, observed, or inferred;
- whether a second source is needed;
- the page section that will use it.

Source priority for Wingstop content:

1. Wingstop official menu, ordering flow, nutrition, allergen, newsroom, investor-media, and location pages.
2. Government or regulatory sources for health, nutrition, or legal claims.
3. Original research, direct testing, or clearly documented observations.
4. Reputable secondary sources when the primary source does not answer the question.

Competitors are gap-discovery sources, not factual authorities. Never copy their wording, structure, opinions, tables, or unsupported numbers.

Verify time-sensitive facts on the day of writing. These include prices, flavors, promotions, piece counts, included sides, nutrition data, allergens, store counts, ordering options, and limited-time availability.

If a fact cannot be verified:

- omit it;
- qualify it as an estimate or observation with the basis stated; or
- explain that the selected restaurant or current official ordering cart is the final source.

Never invent prices, calories, ratings, survey results, quotes, customer sentiment, popularity claims, dates, credentials, tests, or first-hand experience.

## 5. Evidence, Experience, and Trust

Trust is the most important part of E-E-A-T. Demonstrate it through accurate claims, clear limits, original work, ownership transparency, and corrections.

- Use first-hand language only when the writer or site completed the stated test, order, visit, calculation, or comparison and evidence exists.
- Do not write "we tested," "our experts found," or similar claims without a real documented method.
- Do not invent a named author, credential, reviewer, or expert quote.
- Explain the method when the page contains calculations, rankings, taste judgments, or comparisons.
- State conflicts, uncertainty, regional variation, and exceptions where they affect the decision.
- Keep the independent-site and non-affiliation disclosure accurate.

Integrate a source link near a consequential claim when it helps readers verify the information. Do not add a generic visible "Sources and research note" block unless the user requests it or the page genuinely benefits from a reference list. Maintain internal source provenance even when a public source list is not appropriate.

## 6. Writing Standard

Write in clear, natural U.S. English for an ordinary customer. Sound informed and useful, not corporate, academic, salesy, or machine-generated.

### Structure

- Use one descriptive H1 that matches the page's main purpose.
- Use H2 and H3 headings that tell the reader what the section answers. Question headings are useful when they reflect a real query; do not force every heading into a question.
- Use an answer-first approach for genuine questions. Give the direct answer first, then evidence, variables, and practical advice.
- A concise answer block may support snippets and AI citations, but write it for the reader. Do not force every answer into a fixed 40–60-word range.
- Keep each section self-contained enough to be understood when quoted, without repeating the same introduction across sections.
- Use short paragraphs when they improve scanning. Vary paragraph and sentence length naturally.
- Use tables for exact comparisons, checklists for actions, and decision trees for choices with multiple variables. Do not decorate simple facts with unnecessary components.

### Human prose

- Name the product, action, actor, condition, and outcome.
- Prefer active voice, but use natural passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Do not chase a percentage.
- Mix sentence lengths and paragraph shapes.
- Use light humor only when it fits the topic and cannot undermine accuracy, safety, or trust.
- Write transitions that follow the logic of the subject, not a template.
- Replace generic labels such as "The Quick Answer" with a useful heading or pair the label with the exact reader question.

Avoid:

- "In today's fast-paced world," "deep dive," "game-changer," "ultimate guide," and similar filler;
- robotic setups such as "Here is the thing" or "It is important to note";
- fake drama, repeated binary contrasts, canned conclusions, and rhetorical questions used as decoration;
- em dashes, stacked fragments, and three-part lists used in every section;
- claims such as "best," "most popular," "fan favorite," or "best value" without a stated basis;
- claiming text is "100% human written" or trying to defeat AI detectors;
- placeholder headings, placeholder copy, dummy statistics, and template notes in public HTML.

Before publication, read the page aloud and run a prose review for filler, repeated sentence patterns, vague claims, and unsupported certainty.

## 7. Keyword and Intent Rules

Start with the reader's search intent, not keyword insertion.

- Select one primary topic or query cluster per page.
- Use secondary terms only when they represent a distinct sub-question the page answers.
- Put the main topic naturally in the title, H1, introduction, and a relevant heading when the wording fits.
- Use entities, synonyms, attributes, and related concepts because they improve meaning, not to manufacture semantic coverage.
- Do not repeat exact-match phrases to reach a density or tool recommendation.
- Do not create awkward anchors, headings, alt text, or schema fields to hold keywords.
- Read the finished copy without the keyword list. If wording sounds unnatural, rewrite it.

Semrush metrics may help identify demand, intent, difficulty, and overused terms. Label unavailable or estimated metrics. Never confuse traffic, downloads, impressions, or tool estimates with verified search volume.

## 8. AI Search and Citation Readiness

AI-search optimization uses the same foundation as people-first SEO. Do not create separate thin pages for every prompt variation.

For citation-ready sections:

- answer a precise question in direct language;
- define the scope and date of time-sensitive claims;
- keep names, quantities, units, and conditions explicit;
- place supporting evidence close to the claim;
- use semantic HTML such as headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, and descriptive links;
- add original comparisons, calculations, or decision support when evidence allows;
- make important content visible in the page, not available only through scripts or decorative CSS.

Do not promise AI citations, Discover traffic, snippets, rankings, or rich results.

## 9. Article Depth Workflow

When asked to improve article depth without rewriting existing content, suggest only new sections. Do not modify the draft unless the user separately authorizes it.

Each proposed section must include:

- an H2 or H3 heading;
- the reader problem it solves;
- why existing coverage commonly misses it, based on inspected evidence rather than a made-up percentage;
- why it adds experience, trust, semantic relevance, or decision value;
- four or five concrete points to cover;
- the new conclusion or decision the reader can make;
- the best format: table, checklist, comparison, case study, framework, steps, decision tree, or expert note.

Prioritize real gaps such as edge cases, store variation, exceptions, conflicting evidence, failure conditions, cross-contact, price-channel differences, substitutions, and situations where common advice changes with the variables.

Add a myth-versus-reality section only when specific, evidenced misconceptions exist. Add an advanced section only when there is useful material beyond the basics. Do not manufacture novelty.

## 10. Competitor Gap Analysis

Use competitor analysis to improve the reader experience, not to imitate the competing page.

For a supplied page and competitor:

1. Inspect both current pages.
2. State what a first-time visitor understands from each in the first 10 seconds: purpose, audience, answer, freshness, and next action.
3. Identify one verifiable trust signal on the competitor that the site lacks.
4. Quote only short, necessary excerpts from both pages and link to the source. Respect copyright limits.
5. Recommend one specific addition that closes the reader or trust gap.

Ignore raw word count, keyword density, and backlink totals unless the user explicitly asks for them. Do not assume that an outranking page ranks because of one visible factor. Separate observations from inferences.

Keep competitor names and copied language out of public articles unless the page is an authorized comparison page. Store research in internal briefs or provenance notes.

## 11. Reviews, Rankings, and Comparisons

Review content needs original evidence and a declared method. A summary of manufacturer copy or other reviews is not a review.

For a review or ranked list:

- identify who the recommendation suits and who should avoid it;
- state the evaluation criteria and how each item was assessed;
- show original evidence from use, testing, photographs, receipts, calculations, or a reproducible research method;
- discuss benefits, drawbacks, tradeoffs, and meaningful alternatives;
- explain which variables can change the conclusion;
- distinguish personal taste from measurable facts;
- disclose affiliate relationships, free products, sponsorships, or other conflicts;
- avoid fake star ratings, aggregate ratings, votes, or review schema.

Use Review or Product structured data only when the visible page and evidence meet Google's current eligibility and content rules.

## 12. On-Page and Technical Requirements

Every indexable article must have:

- a unique, descriptive, concise `<title>`;
- a useful meta description that accurately summarizes the page without keyword repetition;
- one visible H1 aligned with the title and primary intent;
- a self-referencing canonical URL;
- `index, follow, max-image-preview:large` unless a reviewed indexing decision says otherwise;
- crawlable internal links using real `<a href>` elements and descriptive anchor text;
- a relevant entry in `sitemap.xml` with an honest `lastmod` date when the page is new or substantially updated;
- mobile-readable, accessible HTML with no horizontal overflow;
- a relevant image when it helps the reader, using a descriptive filename, accurate alt text, explicit dimensions, and an optimized WebP or AVIF file;
- a representative `og:image`, preferably at least 1200 pixels wide for Discover-oriented articles;
- structured data that matches all visible content and passes JSON parsing and the relevant validation tool.

Do not hide substantive article content in accordions merely for design. FAQs may use accessible disclosure controls, but the answers must exist in the HTML and work without relying on fabricated schema.

FAQ, Q&A, Article, Product, Review, Offer, and other schema types are not interchangeable. Check current Google eligibility before adding them. Structured data does not guarantee a rich result.

## 13. Google Discover Rules

Discover eligibility requires indexing and compliance; it never guarantees distribution.

- Use accurate, non-clickbait titles and previews.
- Avoid sensationalism, withheld context, and exaggerated claims.
- Prefer timely stories, strong original insight, or useful evergreen content with renewed relevance.
- Use a relevant, high-quality image at least 1200 pixels wide, ideally suitable for a 16:9 preview.
- Avoid generic logos and text-heavy hero images.
- Keep `max-image-preview:large` enabled.
- Maintain a fast, stable, mobile-friendly page experience.

Do not change a date to create fake freshness. Update the date only when the page received a substantive factual or editorial update.

## 14. Publication Gate

Do not publish until all applicable checks pass:

### Research

- Primary and time-sensitive claims have current sources.
- Prices, calories, availability, and allergens are correctly scoped.
- Inferences and estimates are labeled.
- No unverifiable claim remains.

### Content

- The page fully satisfies one primary intent.
- The opening answers the reader's task without filler.
- Every section adds new information or decision value.
- The writing sounds natural when read aloud.
- No placeholder, template note, unsupported superlative, or competitor copy remains.

### SEO and markup

- Title, description, H1, canonical, headings, and internal links are correct.
- Visible content and schema agree exactly.
- The page is linked from a relevant existing page.
- The sitemap entry exists and uses an honest `lastmod`.
- Images have accurate alt text, dimensions, and optimized files.

### QA

- Validate HTML or parse the document for structural errors.
- Parse every JSON-LD block.
- Test links, FAQ controls, and images.
- Check at mobile and desktop widths for overflow and readability.
- Copy and use the exact current shared header and footer markup, navigation links, CSS classes, colors, spacing, and mobile behavior from an approved existing page. A visually similar or simplified replacement is not acceptable. Content-page styling must not override or redesign the shared header or footer. Change the shared header or footer only when the user explicitly requests a site-wide header or footer change.
- After deployment, verify the production URL, sitemap, canonical, images, and Vercel status.
